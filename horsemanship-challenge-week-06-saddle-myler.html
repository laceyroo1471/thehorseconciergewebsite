About
Toklat and Myler

This week is presented by Toklat in partnership with Myler.

Since 1976, Toklat has served the equestrian community with quality products for English, Western, and endurance riders. Based in Oregon, Toklat manufactures Myler bits and works closely with the Myler team to bring its innovative bitting designs and educational resources to riders.

The Myler Bitting System was developed by renowned horsemen and educators Bob and Dale Myler. Rather than viewing a bit as simply a means of control, the Myler philosophy focuses on comfort, relaxation, and clearer communication between horse and rider. Its progressive Level system considers the horse’s anatomy, disposition, experience, and stage of training to help determine the appropriate balance of tongue pressure and tongue relief.

Together, Toklat and Myler combine thoughtful design, quality manufacturing, practical education, and personalized bitting assistance to help riders make more informed choices for their individual horses.

    Complete the quiz on this week’s lesson · up to 15 pts

    For non-riders, and anyone who wants the lesson check. Fictional horse: Scout is a calm, experienced trail horse. He frequently tosses his head, opens his mouth, and goes behind the bit when rein pressure is applied. He currently wears a single-jointed mouthpiece with a 4-inch shank and a 2-inch purchase. Each correct answer is 2 points. Both written answers together are 5 points.

    1. Recognizing resistance. Which detail most strongly suggests that Scout’s behavior could be related to bit resistance?

    2. Tongue pressure. Why can excessive tongue pressure cause resistance?

    3. Understanding Myler Levels. Which statement is correct?

    4. Applying the leverage scale. Scout’s bit has a 4-inch shank and a 2-inch purchase. What is its shank-to-purchase ratio, and what does that mean?

    5. Comparing two bits. Two bits have identical shank cheekpieces. One has a broken mouthpiece, while the other has a solid ported mouthpiece. Which statement is correct?

    Put the week together

    Using your chosen horse or the Scout scenario, answer both questions in at least three complete sentences each.
