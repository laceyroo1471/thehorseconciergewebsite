    Monday November 9

    Emergency Prep for Horse Owners

    Dr. Rebecca Husted on what preparation actually looks like — then name the person who steps in if you cannot.

    Content unlocks Monday, November 9.

    From Dr. Rebecca Husted

    The person you designate should be able to reach the barn, the horses, and the decisions that cannot wait — even if they never install the app.

    Emergency handoff · 5 + 5 points

    Create the handoff in the app for 5 points. It does not have to be finished.

    Share it with your designee for another 5 points. They do not have to accept. They only have to receive it.

    Tuesday November 10

    Calling 911

    A short interview with Tori McLeod — what to tell dispatch, which resources to ask for, and what to do while help is coming.

    Content unlocks Tuesday, November 10.

    Interview coming

    Tori McLeod of 4 Hooves Large Animal Services. The video will be added here once the interview is confirmed. Watching does not add points.

    The conversation covers what to tell dispatch, how to describe the location and the scene, which resources to request, what happens after the call, and what to do while help is on the way — including what you can learn about local capabilities before you ever need them.

    Wednesday November 11

    Barn Fires

    Latches, exits, and the route you would actually use — then walk it, and name two hazards in your own barn.

    Content unlocks Wednesday, November 11.

    What to watch for

    Go past the obvious flammables. Notice how fast smoke moves, latches that take two hands, exits a responder cannot find, and the gathering point you would actually reach.

    Walk your evacuation route · 5 points

    This is a baseline, not a race. Start where you would actually be. Halter the horse. Follow the exit you would use. Stop at the gathering point.

    Track it with GPS in the app during this week. In the activity notes, write the wasted steps or delays you noticed. The note is what completes the 5 points.

    Two fire hazards · 5 points

    Name two things in your barn that would slow a fire evacuation or a responder. A photo is welcome. The two written hazards are what earn the points. Photos together need to stay under 10 MB.

    Thursday November 12

    Evacuation

    Where you would actually go, how the horses get there, and what happens if you are not the one who has to leave.

    Content unlocks Thursday, November 12.

    From Dr. Rebecca Husted

    A trailer and a destination are the start. The plan has to survive an alternate place to go, more than one horse, and a day when you are not there to load them.

    Start an evacuation plan · 10 points

    Open the evacuation plan in the app and start it. Starting is the whole 10 points. It does not have to be a finished, ready-to-execute plan.

    Friday November 13

    Entrapments and Rescue

    A short interview with Tori McLeod on mud, water, and a horse that is down — what owners should and should not do.

    Content unlocks Friday, November 13.

    Interview coming

    Tori McLeod of 4 Hooves Large Animal Services. The video will be added here once the interview is confirmed. Watching does not add points.

    The lane is mud, water, and a horse that cannot get itself up: what is happening to the horse, why quiet can mean exhaustion, how to keep people safe, and what to leave to a professional rescue.

    Saturday November 14

    Week Review

    Five questions from this week. Each correct answer is 4 points.

    Content unlocks Saturday, November 14.

    Five questions · 4 points each

    Stay signed in. If Tuesday’s or Friday’s interview is not up yet, the question still has what you need to choose. A wrong answer does not award points, and you will see the right one.

    1. Emergency handoff. An owner is hospitalized and cannot communicate. Their designated caregiver discovers that a horse’s medication instructions in the app differ from the prescription label. What is the best next step?

    2. Calling 911. A horse is trapped in a drainage ditch. You have called 911, provided the location, and described the situation. The dispatcher asks whether the horse is standing or lying down. Why might this information matter?

    3. Barn fire. A barn has smoke detectors, accessible exits, and halters near every stall. During a walkthrough, you discover that several stall latches require two hands and a precise lifting motion. What is the primary concern?

    4. Emergency evacuation. A horse owner has confirmed transportation for all six horses and identified an evacuation destination. What additional issue could make the plan unworkable?

    5. Horse entrapment and rescue. According to the technical rescue principles described by Dr. Rebecca Gimenez Husted, why might rescuers reposition a recumbent horse rather than attempt to lift it completely?
