About
The Equine Institute

At The Equine Institute, our mission is to help horses by educating the people who care for them.

We believe that by equipping people like you — horse owners, enthusiasts, and equine professionals — with the knowledge to care for horses like Braveheart better, we can directly improve the health and well-being of horses everywhere. We want to remove the unknown and the intimidation, and make you confident and curious to learn more.

The Equine Institute came to be from a vision to combine scientific research with practical care, and make it easy to access. Horses everywhere deserve the best care, and we believe our products can create that world for you and them.

What sets us apart

  • Expert-led instruction. Our courses are developed and taught by industry professionals, including veterinarians, physiotherapists, and experienced equine bodyworkers.
  • Science-based approach. We combine traditional practices with cutting-edge research to offer the most effective equine care methods.
  • Accredited education. Many of our courses offer continuing education credits, approved by respected organizations like PATH Intl, RACE, NBCAAM, and ETAA.
  • Inclusive learning. Whether you’re a beginner or a seasoned professional, our courses meet you where you are and help you grow.

    Whether you’re a lifelong horse owner or just beginning your equine journey, understanding why horses move, perform, and sometimes become unsound starts with a solid foundation in anatomy, biomechanics, and conformation.

    This week, you’ll discover fascinating concepts that most horse owners were never taught, including why horses don’t have a collarbone, how the hindquarter muscles generate power, what the walk can reveal about soundness, and how subtle differences in conformation can influence long-term performance and health. We’ll wrap up the week with a real clinical case study that shows how all of these concepts come together in practice.

    Each lesson is a small excerpt from one of Equine Institute’s comprehensive veterinary-developed courses. Our mission is to make professional-level equine education accessible to every horse owner through engaging, science-based learning created by veterinarians and experienced equine professionals. We hope this week gives you a new way of looking at your horse, and leaves you wanting to learn even more.

    Tuesday September 8

    Meet Your Horse’s Three Hamstrings

    Most riders talk about “the hamstrings.” Horses have three major ones — each with a different job for impulsion and soundness.

    Content unlocks Tuesday, September 8.

    Lesson PDF

    The biceps femoris is the largest and most lateral. The semitendinosus sits farther back and helps power the hip, stifle, and hock. The semimembranosus lies on the inside of the hind limb. Together they generate the power that propels the horse forward.

    The Hind End: Where Soundness Is Won or Lost

    Start with the hock angle — sickle hocks and post-legged horses carry very different long-term risks.

    Content unlocks Friday, September 11.

    Lesson PDF

    Ideally the hind cannon is nearly perpendicular to the ground. Too much hock angulation (sickle hocks) stresses ligaments. Too little (post-legged) reduces shock absorption and raises arthritis and locking-stifle risk.

    Saturday September 12

    Case Study · Fibrotic Myopathy

    A mare with bilateral fibrotic myopathy — watch how scarred hamstrings change the hind-leg swing.

    Content unlocks Saturday, September 12.

    What to watch for

    This mare has bilateral fibrotic myopathy, a mechanical lameness from scar tissue in the hamstrings. Her hind legs swing forward, then snap abruptly to the ground. The injury is non-painful once healed, but it restricts elasticity and changes how she moves.

    Look for the movement pattern, what caused the scar tissue, how it affects soundness, and how the condition is managed — then connect it back to Tuesday’s hamstrings and Friday’s hind-end lesson.
