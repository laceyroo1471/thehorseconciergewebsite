About
CRS Horseshoes

Riley Henson, CJF, is a lifelong farrier who started CRS Horseshoes in Dublin, Texas after he was dissatisfied with the results he saw on lameness cases. That search became a line of composite therapeutic and performance shoes — and the slogan Helping People, Helping Horses.

Traditional shoes lock a foot to one medial/lateral plane. CRS shoes are built so the hoof can self-adjust, with a focus on navicular, ringbone, and soft-tissue cases that do not always respond to conventional shoeing.

This week’s lessons cover how to read a hoof, distortion, laminitis, abscesses, and thrush.

    Deciphering the Language

    Content unlocks Monday, September 14.

    Before we get into hoof evaluation, distortion, and lameness, we’re starting with the language.

    You’ve probably heard many of these terms for years — HPA, DDFT, medial, lateral, breakover, ground reaction force — and you may know some better than others.

    Work through the Decoder Cards below. You don’t need to memorize them. The goal is to understand what the terms mean and why they matter so the rest of this week’s education makes more sense.

    Open a library, swipe through its cards, then come back for the next shelf. When you are finished, answer today’s 5-point question.

    Decoder question · 5 points

    After you have worked through the cards, answer this one question. Points are awarded only for the correct answer.

    When a horse has a broken-back hoof-pastern axis, which relationship is being described?

    Abscesses & Thrush

    One often makes a horse suddenly lame. The other lives in a packed, wet frog. Both change how a foot lands.

    Content unlocks Friday, September 18.

    Abscess guide PDF

    A sudden, severe lameness with a stronger digital pulse in one foot is a classic abscess picture. Use this guide with today’s video.

    Download Hoof Abscess PDF

    Thrush

    Thrush is an infection of the frog that thrives when the foot stays wet or packed. Watch for a black, foul-smelling discharge in the central sulcus.

    Earn 10 points

    Track a hand walk with GPS in the app and add a note on how the hooves land. The walk alone does not count — the landing note is required. Points score automatically from the app.

    • Does one foot land harder, flatter, or more toe-first?
    • Is the landing even from left to right?
    • Do you see anything that matches this week’s distortion or abscess lessons?
